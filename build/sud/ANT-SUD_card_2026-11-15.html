Scope: OBED triage (any GA, not in labor) and L&D/intrapartum/postpartum inpatient care of a pregnant or postpartum patient (≤12 wk) who reports, screens positive for, or presents with intoxication, withdrawal, overdose or complications of methamphetamine/other stimulants, opioids (fentanyl, heroin, nonprescribed pills, and patients on MOUD — buprenorphine or methadone), cocaine, alcohol, or cannabis (including cannabinoid hyperemesis). Companion algorithm: FD-ANT-SUD. Severe BP or preeclampsia picture → ANT-04 in parallel (this card stays open as the substance card). Sepsis (e.g., injection-related infection) → SYS-03 first. Cardiac arrest/overdose arrest → SYS-04. Nausea/vomiting without cannabis → ANT-08. Excludes tobacco/nicotine, benzodiazepine use disorder (consult; brief notes only), and neonatal treatment beyond the handoff (peds owns the newborn). Doses live only in the DOSES section of this card.

BOTTOM LINE

  • Substance use in pregnancy is a medical condition. Screen EVERY patient verbally with a validated tool; do not use urine drug testing as the screen (ACOG CO 711, CO 633, CC No. 10).
  • Local policy: urine drug testing (UDS) only for a clinical question and only with informed consent — tell her why, that she may decline, and what a positive result can lead to (including newborn team/social work/DFPS involvement). Screening immunoassays are presumptive — confirm before acting on a positive (ACOG Statement of Policy 2020/2024; CO 711).
  • OPIOIDS: NEVER stop, taper, or "hold for labor" buprenorphine or methadone. Continue the verified home dose through labor, cesarean and postpartum. Never give nalbuphine or butorphanol to a patient on MOUD or with OUD (precipitated withdrawal). Treat opioid withdrawal promptly (COWS). Every OUD patient leaves with a naloxone kit.
  • METHAMPHETAMINE/COCAINE intoxication can look exactly like severe preeclampsia or abruption (hypertension, headache, tachycardia, agitation, abdominal pain, FHR changes). Treat the BP and evaluate for preeclampsia/abruption as if no drug were involved (ANT-04); benzodiazepines first for agitation and sympathomimetic toxicity. Local policy: after recent cocaine/meth, lorazepam (Ativan) IV is first-line for hypertension and agitation, repeated as needed (see DOSES); severe-range BP → nifedipine or hydralazine as needed on the ANT-04 ladders. Avoid beta-blockers, including labetalol, during acute stimulant toxicity.
  • STIMULANT WITHDRAWAL (e.g., 3rd-trimester methamphetamine): no FDA-approved medication; supportive care. It is not physiologically dangerous like alcohol or opioid withdrawal — the dangers are depression and SUICIDALITY (screen), hypersomnia mistaken for oversedation, poor intake, and return to use.
  • ALCOHOL withdrawal in pregnancy: local policy — any pregnant patient in alcohol withdrawal is admitted (ASAM 2020: consider inpatient for all). CIWA-Ar; benzodiazepines are first-line (short-acting near term/preterm risk); thiamine.
  • CANNABIS: no safe amount and no medical indication in pregnancy. Persistent cyclic vomiting with relief from hot showers in a daily user = consider cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome (CHS). A positive cannabis UDS alone is not by itself grounds for a report.
  • TEXAS: substance use during pregnancy is not, by itself, a mandated report to law enforcement. Texas Family Code ch. 261 abuse/neglect reporting (to DFPS within 48 h for professionals) applies to a CHILD — i.e., after birth. Newborn substance exposure is handled through social work, DFPS intake and the CAPTA/CARA Plan of Safe Care process. Always consult case management/social work for hospital and community policy.
  • Every positive screen or known SUD: social work/case management consult; anesthesia early if admitted for delivery; peds/NICU notified before birth.

RED FLAGS → CALL OB ATTENDING NOW (and rapid response / SYS-04 as indicated)

Opioid overdose (RR <10 or apnea, SpO2 <90%, pinpoint pupils, unresponsive) → airway/ventilation + naloxone (see DOSES) + SYS-04 if pulseless. Severe-range BP, chest pain, arrhythmia, wide QRS, seizure, hyperthermia, stroke symptoms, severe agitation or psychosis, suicidal ideation; alcohol withdrawal with CIWA-Ar ≥10, hallucinations, seizure or delirium tremens; abruption signs (painful bleeding, tetanic/tachysystolic contractions, rigid uterus); abnormal FHR/Category II–III; signs of injection-related infection (fever, endocarditis murmur, abscess) → SYS-03 screen.

UNIVERSAL STEPS (every patient on this card)

  1. Vitals with the maternal early warning tool (use your unit's thresholds); GA/viability; FHT by Doppler (<20 wk) or continuous EFM/toco (viable GA) while intoxicated, withdrawing, hypertensive, or with abdominal pain/bleeding.
  2. Verbal screen with a validated tool — 4Ps, NIDA Quick Screen, or CRAFFT if ≤26 y (ACOG CO 711). Ask about every substance (polysubstance is common), route (injection), last use, fentanyl exposure, MOUD (name, dose, prescriber or OTP, last dose, take-home doses), alcohol pattern, prior withdrawal seizures/DTs, mental health and suicidality, IPV, housing, other children at home.
  3. Verify MOUD: buprenorphine — Texas PMP + prescriber; methadone — call the OTP for dose and last dosed date (OTP records are 42 CFR Part 2 protected; use the local release process).
  4. Labs as clinically indicated: CBC, CMP (LFTs), magnesium; preeclampsia labs (ANT-04) if BP elevated; ECG if stimulant intoxication, chest pain, or methadone (QTc); troponin if chest pain. Infectious screening if not done this pregnancy or ongoing risk: HIV, HBsAg, HCV antibody/RNA, syphilis, GC/CT (see ANT-STI).
  5. UDS ONLY with informed consent and a clinical question (see below).
  6. Social work/case management consult for every positive screen or known SUD.

SCREENING AND URINE DRUG TESTING — CONSENT

  • Screening = conversation/validated questionnaire, done universally and equally (ACOG CO 633: routine laboratory testing of biologic samples is not required). Selective testing based on appearance, race, or insurance introduces bias (ACOG CC No. 10; ACOG Statement of Policy).
  • If a test is clinically indicated (altered mental status, unexplained HTN/tachycardia, abruption, seizure, overdose): obtain and document informed consent — indication, right to refuse, consequences of refusal, and possible outcomes of a positive result (ACOG Statement of Policy 2020, reaffirmed 2024). If she lacks capacity (unresponsive), test for emergency management and disclose afterward.
  • Interpretation: immunoassay results are presumptive. False positives occur (e.g., amphetamine screen with some medications); opiate screens do not reliably detect fentanyl, buprenorphine, or methadone — specific assays are needed. Send confirmatory testing (GC/MS or LC-MS/MS) before any result is used for reporting or non-urgent decisions. A negative screen does not rule out use. Cross-reactivity questions → the lab.
  • Newborn testing (urine, meconium, cord) is a peds/NICU decision under local policy; ACOG calls for parental informed consent before testing the neonate. ACOG opposes "test and report."

OBED TRIAGE (any GA, not in labor; postpartum ≤12 wk)

a) Methamphetamine / amphetamine (intoxication)

  • Picture: HTN, tachycardia, hyperthermia, agitation, psychosis, chest pain, dilated pupils, insomnia, poor intake/weight loss, dental disease; abruption, preterm labor, fetal growth restriction (lower birth weight in meta-analysis).
  • Preeclampsia look-alike: meth hypertension often cannot be distinguished from gestational hypertension/preeclampsia (ASAM/AAAP 2024). Manage as hypertension in pregnancy per ANT-04 (labs, severe-range BP treatment, magnesium if preeclampsia with severe features is possible). Do not attribute BP to meth and discharge without the preeclampsia workup.
  • Agitation/hyperadrenergic state: calm environment; local policy — lorazepam first-line (ASAM/AAAP 2024: benzodiazepine first-line) → see DOSES. Psychosis not responding → antipsychotic (psychiatry). Hyperthermia → active cooling.
  • Hypertension: local policy — lorazepam first (see DOSES), then nifedipine or hydralazine as needed for severe-range or persistent BP on the ANT-04 ladders (ASAM/AAAP 2024 names dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers for hypertensive emergency). Magnesium if preeclampsia with severe features is possible. Avoid beta-blockers, including labetalol, during acute toxicity.
  • Fetal (local policy): continuous EFM while hypertensive/intoxicated at viable GA; abruption watch (bleeding, tachysystole, Cat II/III); growth US if none in the past 3–4 wk.
  • Disposition: admit if severe-range BP, preeclampsia not excluded, abruption concern, psychosis, suicidality, or unable to care for self. Home only when sober, normotensive, preeclampsia excluded, reassuring fetal status, safety plan and follow-up.

b) Methamphetamine / stimulant WITHDRAWAL (including third trimester)

  • Picture: "crash" — hypersomnia (often 12–24 h of somnolence), fatigue, increased appetite, dysphoria/depression, anxiety, irritability, craving; post-acute symptoms (depression, insomnia, paranoia) can last weeks to months (ASAM/AAAP 2024).
  • No FDA-approved pharmacotherapy for stimulant withdrawal; a Cochrane review (2009, 4 RCTs) found no effective medication. Management is SUPPORTIVE: safe quiet setting, sleep, hydration and nutrition, symptom relief (see DOSES for adjuncts), psychosocial support. Address co-occurring opioid/alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal, which ARE dangerous.
  • SUICIDALITY: screen every patient with the suicide-risk tool (e.g., C-SSRS); risk may rise as intoxication wanes and in acute withdrawal. Positive → 1:1/sitter per local policy and psychiatry.
  • Hypersomnia vs oversedation: stimulant crash sleepiness can be mistaken for opioid/methadone oversedation — check RR, SpO2, pupils, arousability before giving or holding medicines.
  • Fetal risk: there is no evidence that stimulant withdrawal itself harms the fetus; the fetal risks come from return to use, maternal hypertension/abruption, poor nutrition, and maternal self-harm (ASAM/AAAP; expert consensus).
  • Fetal monitoring (local policy): viable GA → NST on presentation; continuous EFM if recent use within ~24–48 h, HTN, contractions, bleeding, or abdominal pain; growth US + amniotic fluid if none in 3–4 wk; weekly NST/BPP only for an obstetric indication (FGR, HTN), not for stimulant exposure alone.
  • Do not deliver for withdrawal alone; deliver for obstetric indications.

c) Opioids — OUD, MOUD, withdrawal, overdose

  • On MOUD (buprenorphine or methadone): CONTINUE the verified dose. Never taper or stop acutely in pregnancy (ACOG CO 711; SAMHSA 2018; SOAP/SMFM/ASRA 2025). Pregnancy may increase metabolism — split dosing may be needed in the 3rd trimester (coordinate with prescriber/OTP; see DOSES).
  • Do not switch methadone → buprenorphine in pregnancy (precipitated withdrawal risk; ACOG CO 711); any switch is a low-dose initiation with addiction medicine.
  • Withdrawal recognition: COWS (yawning, rhinorrhea, lacrimation, piloerection, mydriasis, sweats, myalgia, GI upset, restlessness, anxiety, tachycardia). Treat promptly — withdrawal may cause fetal distress and increases preterm labor risk. Stimulant intoxication mimics opioid withdrawal (mydriasis, sweating, agitation, HTN); rhinorrhea, yawning and piloerection point to opioids.
  • Medically supervised withdrawal ("detox") is NOT recommended over MOUD: low completion and high relapse (Obstet Gynecol 2018;131:803–14).
  • Local policy: OBED may start buprenorphine (untreated OUD, wants treatment):
    • Standard start when COWS is >8 (mild–moderate objective withdrawal) and enough time since the last full agonist → see DOSES; discharge on a bridge prescription to confirmed follow-up.
    • FENTANYL use: standard starts carry a higher risk of precipitated withdrawal. Low-dose ("micro") initiation while continuing a full agonist is preferred → ADMIT for inpatient initiation (complex schedule; pharmacy splits films) → see DOSES. Addiction medicine/pharmacy consult when available.
    • Precipitated withdrawal: more buprenorphine, or return to the tolerated full agonist and restart slowly → see DOSES.
    • Methadone can be started for OUD only in a hospital for stabilization/withdrawal or by an OTP; ongoing outpatient methadone requires an OTP.
    • UDS is NOT required to start buprenorphine.
  • Mixed agonist-antagonists (nalbuphine, butorphanol) and pentazocine: AVOID in OUD and on MOUD — precipitated withdrawal (ACOG CO 711; NPMS bundle). Local policy: enter them in the EHR allergy/intolerance field.
  • Overdose: ventilation first, then naloxone titrated in small IV aliquots to restore breathing without precipitating severe withdrawal; buprenorphine overdose may need higher naloxone doses; observe — naloxone is shorter-acting than many opioids → see DOSES. EFM after maternal stabilization at viable GA.
  • Discharge from OBED: buprenorphine prescription bridging to a confirmed follow-up (prescriber, OTP or clinic), naloxone kit IN HAND, overdose education (fentanyl/xylazine, not using alone), prenatal follow-up, social work referral. Any clinician with a standard DEA registration may prescribe buprenorphine (X-waiver eliminated; ACOG Clinical Practice Update Sept 2025).

d) Cocaine (intoxication)

  • Picture: HTN, tachycardia, chest pain/ischemia, arrhythmia, seizure, agitation, hyperthermia, stroke; ABRUPTION (classic association — treat any abdominal pain/bleeding as abruption until proven otherwise), preterm labor, FGR.
  • First-line for agitation, tachycardia and hypertension driven by sympathomimetic toxicity: BENZODIAZEPINES (ASAM/AAAP 2024) → see DOSES.
  • Local policy: lorazepam first-line (see DOSES); severe-range BP → nifedipine or hydralazine as needed on the ANT-04 ladders. Avoid beta-blockers, including labetalol, in acute cocaine toxicity ("unopposed alpha" concern); never metoprolol, propranolol or esmolol. CCB/vasodilators preferred for ischemia.
  • Chest pain: ECG, troponin, cardiology; nitroglycerin for ischemia; evaluate for ACS/aortic dissection.
  • Wide QRS/arrhythmia: sodium-channel effect — poison center (1-800-222-1222) and cardiology.
  • Fetal: continuous EFM at viable GA; abruption labs (CBC, fibrinogen, coags) if pain/bleeding.

e) Alcohol

  • Any alcohol use in pregnancy: no known safe amount; counsel on fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD); brief intervention and referral.
  • Withdrawal: onset ~6–24 h after last drink; tremor, sweats, tachycardia, HTN, anxiety, nausea, insomnia; seizures; hallucinations; delirium tremens. Score with CIWA-Ar — valid in pregnancy; nausea, headache, anxiety and insomnia should be attributed to withdrawal rather than pregnancy (ASAM 2020).
  • Level of care: local policy — any pregnant patient in alcohol withdrawal is admitted (ASAM 2020: consider inpatient for all pregnant patients needing withdrawal management; offer for CIWA-Ar ≥10). Obstetric consultation during withdrawal management.
  • Medications: benzodiazepines are the drugs of choice in pregnancy; fetal risks appear small and are outweighed by severe-withdrawal risk (ASAM 2020). Short-acting benzodiazepine in the late 3rd trimester or with preterm-delivery risk. NO valproic acid → see DOSES.
  • Thiamine to prevent Wernicke encephalopathy (IV/IM if poor nutrition); thiamine and glucose may be given in any order or together — never delay glucose for hypoglycemia (ASAM 2020) → see DOSES.
  • Newborn: if withdrawal was treated near delivery, tell peds (neonatal benzodiazepine effects/sedative withdrawal, FASD assessment).

f) Cannabis (including cannabinoid hyperemesis)

  • No safe amount; no medical indication in pregnancy/postpartum; associations with preterm birth, low birth weight, NICU admission, and neurodevelopmental effects (ACOG CC No. 10, 2025). Counsel cessation; ask what she is using it for (nausea, anxiety, sleep, pain) and offer alternatives (ANT-08 for NVP).
  • CHS: chronic (usually daily) use, cyclic severe vomiting and abdominal pain, compulsive hot showers/baths for relief, little response to standard antiemetics. Mimics hyperemesis gravidarum — check electrolytes, ketones, LFTs/lipase; consider other causes.
  • Treat: IV fluids/electrolytes per ANT-08; hot showers are safe for comfort. Local policy: if refractory to usual antiemetics, topical capsaicin and/or haloperidol may be added in pregnancy, with ECG/QTc first (SAEM GRACE-4 2024: conditional, very low certainty; AGA CPU 2024) → see DOSES. Definitive treatment = cannabis cessation.
  • Positive cannabis UDS alone: not by itself grounds for a DFPS report in the mother (no child yet) and not, alone, evidence of neglect after birth (DFPS: a positive drug test alone is not evidence of abuse or neglect). Newborn results go through peds/social work.
  • Lactation: discourage use; continued use is NOT a contraindication to breastfeeding — shared decision-making (ACOG CC No. 10; ABM Protocol #21, 2023).

INTRAPARTUM / L&D (admitted for labor, induction, cesarean, or antepartum stay)

  1. On admission: verify MOUD dose and last dose (PMP/OTP); order the home dose — DO NOT hold for labor or NPO status (buprenorphine and methadone continue with sips). Confirm the dose is available from pharmacy.
  2. EARLY ANESTHESIA CONSULT for every patient with OUD/MOUD, recent stimulant/cocaine use, or alcohol withdrawal (NPMS bundle; SOAP/SMFM/ASRA 2025).
  3. Labor analgesia: neuraxial is preferred and encouraged early. Avoid nalbuphine/butorphanol. Expect higher systemic opioid requirements (tolerance, hyperalgesia) — do not under-treat.
  4. Cesarean: continue MOUD; neuraxial anesthesia with neuraxial opioid; scheduled multimodal analgesia (acetaminophen + NSAID if no contraindication — Class 1); consider abdominal wall blocks (TAP/QL) or a 12–24 h epidural; add short-acting full agonists on top of MOUD if needed, with monitoring for oversedation (SOAP/SMFM/ASRA 2025).
  5. Recent methamphetamine/cocaine: anesthesia hemodynamic risk — exaggerated hypertension/tachycardia with laryngoscopy, arrhythmia, possible refractory hypotension with neuraxial or general anesthesia in chronic users, and vasopressor choice (indirect agents such as ephedrine may be less effective; direct agents such as phenylephrine preferred — anesthesia decision). Check platelets/fibrinogen before neuraxial if abruption is suspected.
  6. Hypertension in labor in a stimulant user = preeclampsia until proven otherwise (ANT-04); magnesium per ANT-04 if indicated.
  7. Fetal (local policy): continuous EFM in labor; abruption vigilance; known FGR → peds informed.
  8. Alcohol withdrawal in labor: CIWA-Ar, short-acting benzodiazepine, thiamine (see DOSES); peds aware of benzodiazepine exposure.
  9. Notify peds/NICU on admission (not at delivery): substances, last use, MOUD and dose, UDS results/consent status, alcohol, benzodiazepines, breastfeeding plan (see NEONATAL).
  10. Postpartum: continue MOUD (watch for oversedation as pregnancy metabolism reverses — contact prescriber/OTP before any dose change); scheduled acetaminophen + NSAIDs; limited opioid prescriptions if any; naloxone kit at discharge; prescriber/OTP follow-up arranged BEFORE discharge (postpartum = highest overdose-risk window); contraception offered, including immediate postpartum LARC; depression and suicidality screening; Plan of Safe Care with social work.

SOCIAL WORK AND CASE MANAGEMENT

  • Consult for every positive screen, known SUD, MOUD, or positive/consented UDS — ideally antepartum or on admission, not at discharge. Always consult case management for hospital and community policy.
  • Hospital resources: social work, case management, behavioral health/psychiatry consult, addiction medicine (if available), lactation, chaplain.
  • Community resources: SAMHSA FindTreatment.gov and National Helpline 1-800-662-4357; 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline; Texas HHSC Outreach, Screening, Assessment and Referral (OSAR) centers; local OTP and buprenorphine prescribers; Texas Medicaid postpartum coverage; Arkansas resources for patients living across the state line (case management).
  • Plan of Safe Care elements: MOUD/treatment follow-up, prenatal/postpartum and pediatric follow-up, safe sleep, naloxone, housing/food/transport, ECI referral if indicated, support persons.

TEXAS REPORTING (statute summary, not legal advice — case management/social work for hospital and community policy)

  • Pregnancy: no Texas statute makes a pregnant patient's substance use, by itself, a mandated report to law enforcement. Texas Family Code ch. 261 abuse/neglect reporting concerns a "child" — it applies after birth, not to the fetus.
  • After birth: professionals who have reasonable cause to believe a child has been or may be abused or neglected must report within 48 hours and may not delegate the report (Tex. Fam. Code §261.101(b)). "Abuse" includes current use of a controlled substance in a manner or to an extent that results in physical, mental, or emotional injury to a child (§261.001(1)(I)). Reports are routed through social work.
  • "Born addicted" (Tex. Fam. Code §161.001(a)): a child born to a mother who used a controlled substance (other than one legally obtained by prescription) or alcohol during pregnancy AND who, after birth, shows observable withdrawal, observable harmful effects, or demonstrable presence of the substance in bodily fluids. Prescribed MOUD is excluded; NOWS from prescribed MOUD is an expected, treatable condition.
  • DFPS handles prenatally exposed infants case by case; a parent's positive drug test alone is not evidence of abuse or neglect (DFPS Substance Use Resource Guide). Texas meets the federal CAPTA/CARA Plan of Safe Care requirement through DFPS intake and service-planning tools; hospitals may build a Plan of Safe Care before DFPS involvement.
  • Confidentiality: records from federally assisted SUD programs (OTPs) are covered by 42 CFR Part 2. ACOG: protect confidentiality to the extent the law allows; discourage separation solely for SUD (CO 633); opposes "test and report."

NEONATAL TEAM / NICU NOTIFICATION

  • Notify peds/NICU before delivery for: any opioid exposure including MOUD; methamphetamine/cocaine use this pregnancy; alcohol; benzodiazepines (prescribed or not); cannabis (routine peds awareness); maternal HCV/HIV/HBV status if relevant.
  • NOWS/NAS: expected and treatable after in-utero opioid exposure, including prescribed MOUD — it should not discourage MOUD (ACOG CO 711; AAP 2020). Eat, Sleep, Console: function-based assessment with rooming-in and parental involvement (NEJM 2023 ESC trial: shorter time to medical readiness for discharge without more safety events). Encourage rooming-in, skin-to-skin, and parental presence.
  • Breastfeeding (ABM Protocol #21, 2023; ACOG): stable on MOUD → ENCOURAGED (low milk levels; reduces NOWS severity). Ongoing nonprescribed opioid use → avoid during use. Ongoing methamphetamine/cocaine → avoid breastfeeding (infant deaths reported with methamphetamine). Alcohol → avoid immediately after moderate–high intake; ongoing heavy use → avoid. Cannabis → counsel cessation/reduction; not a contraindication; shared decision-making. Look-back (ABM #21): breastfeeding is recommended when nonprescribed use has stopped by delivery, with ongoing SUD treatment and lactation support.

DRUG CAUTIONS

  • Nalbuphine, butorphanol, pentazocine: avoid in OUD/MOUD (precipitated withdrawal).
  • Beta-blockers, including labetalol, in acute cocaine/methamphetamine toxicity: avoid (local policy); never a pure beta-blocker. Severe-range BP → nifedipine or hydralazine. Once acute toxicity has resolved, standard ANT-04 agent choice applies.
  • Methadone: QTc prolongation — ECG at high doses and with ondansetron/haloperidol; many drug interactions (pharmacy).
  • Ondansetron + methadone or haloperidol: additive QTc — ECG.
  • Benzodiazepines + opioids/MOUD: additive respiratory depression — continuous SpO2, naloxone available.
  • Valproic acid: not for alcohol withdrawal in pregnancy.
  • NSAIDs/ketorolac: avoid at ≥20 wk antepartum; fine postpartum (FDA 2020).
  • Ephedrine may be less effective after chronic stimulant use (anesthesia).

DOSES (to be checked against the pharmacy build)

Opioid overdose:

  • Naloxone IV: 0.1–0.2 mg IV every 2–3 min, titrated to breathing (RR ≥10, SpO2 ≥90%), not to exceed 10 mg; repeated doses or infusion may be needed; higher doses for buprenorphine overdose [WA DOH 2024 hospital guideline; Am J Med Open 2025 review]. Apnea/peri-arrest: per ACLS/SYS-04.
  • Naloxone kit at discharge: naloxone 4 mg nasal spray, 2-pack, with teaching.

Opioid withdrawal / MOUD:

  • Buprenorphine continuation: home SL dose as verified (PMP/prescriber); may split the total daily dose q6–8h for analgesia; usual effective range 16–24 mg/day, max 32 mg/day [Am J Med Open 2025 review].
  • Buprenorphine STANDARD start (COWS >8): 2 mg SL → after 1 h 2 mg SL if tolerated → after 3 h 4 mg SL if tolerated → then 8 mg SL BID; titrate to cravings/withdrawal [institutional example based on ASAM; Am J Med Open 2025 review].
  • Buprenorphine LOW-DOSE start (fentanyl; continue the full agonist meanwhile; inpatient): buccal 150 mcg q6h x2 → 300 mcg q6h x2 → SL 2 mg q6h x2 → SL 4 mg q6h x2 → SL 8 mg BID; stop the full agonist after two 8 mg doses [institutional example, Am J Med Open 2025 review; buccal product or pharmacy-split films per the formulary].
  • Precipitated withdrawal: buprenorphine 2 mg SL every hour until symptoms abate, OR return to the tolerated full agonist dose and restart slowly [Am J Med Open 2025 review].
  • Methadone continuation: OTP-confirmed dose once daily (or split q12h in the 3rd trimester per OTP). If the dose cannot be confirmed: methadone 40 mg may be given plus short-acting opioids for withdrawal until confirmed [Am J Med Open 2025 review]. ECG/QTc at initiation and at higher doses.
  • Short-acting opioid for withdrawal while hospitalized (bridge, not MOUD): e.g., oxycodone 10 mg PO q4h for COWS >8, titrate [Am J Med Open 2025 review].
  • Adjuncts (scheduled 48–96 h for buprenorphine start, or PRN) [WA DOH 2024]: hydroxyzine 50 mg PO q6h; ondansetron 4 mg SL q6h PRN nausea (QTc caution with methadone); loperamide 4 mg PO once then 2 mg PRN loose stools; acetaminophen 650 mg PO q6h PRN; tizanidine 2–4 mg PO q6h; gabapentin 300 mg PO q6h (renal adjust); dicyclomine 20 mg PO q6h; melatonin 3–6 mg PO nightly PRN.
  • Clonidine (autonomic withdrawal symptoms): per the unit order set; hold for hypotension; caution with antihypertensives and magnesium.

Stimulant (methamphetamine/cocaine) intoxication:

  • Stimulant toxicity with hypertension or agitation — local policy: lorazepam (Ativan) 2–4 mg IV first-line, repeat as needed [ASAM/AAAP 2024: benzodiazepine first-line]; titrate to calm with continuous SpO2 monitoring (added respiratory depression with opioids or MOUD).
  • Severe-range BP (confirmed on a recheck within 15 min; treat within 30–60 min of confirmation), nifedipine or hydralazine as needed, on the ANT-04 / Obstetric medicines ladders (CO 767): nifedipine IR PO 10 mg → recheck 20 min → 20 mg → 20 min → 20 mg (swallow whole; never SL or punctured); hydralazine IV 5 or 10 mg over 2 min → recheck 20 min → 10 mg (max 20 mg). Where a ladder's switch step names labetalol, switch to the other of these two agents instead and call the emergency consult. No beta-blockers, including labetalol, during acute toxicity.
  • Methamphetamine withdrawal insomnia/mood: mirtazapine 15 mg PO nightly (off-label; no pregnancy-specific efficacy data) [WA DOH 2024].

Alcohol withdrawal:

  • Diazepam 10 mg PO every hour while CIWA-Ar ≥10 (symptom-triggered front-loading), or fixed 20 mg PO q2h x3 [ASAM 2020]. Late 3rd trimester/preterm-delivery risk: prefer a SHORT-ACTING benzodiazepine (lorazepam per the CIWA order set) [ASAM 2020].
  • Thiamine 100 mg IV/IM daily x3–5 days (IV/IM preferred if malnourished); oral 100 mg daily otherwise; before, with, or after glucose — never delay glucose [ASAM 2020]. Suspected Wernicke: high-dose IV thiamine per pharmacy (ASAM describes 200–500 mg IV q8h).
  • No valproic acid in pregnancy.

Cannabinoid hyperemesis:

  • Haloperidol 0.05 mg/kg IV (0.1 mg/kg was no better and caused more dystonia) [HaVOC RCT, Ann Emerg Med 2021;77:613–9, nonpregnant; GRACE-4 conditional]. ECG/QTc first.
  • Capsaicin 0.1% cream, thin layer to the abdomen (strength per the formulary) [AGA CPU 2024]; avoid mucosa/broken skin; wash hands.
  • Ondansetron/usual antiemetics and IV fluids per ANT-08.

Postpartum/cesarean analgesia with OUD: scheduled acetaminophen + NSAID per the house postpartum order set, on top of continued MOUD.

TEACHING PEARL. The two most dangerous reflexes on L&D with a substance-using patient are holding her buprenorphine or methadone "because she's in labor" and calling a hypertensive methamphetamine user "just the meth." The first produces withdrawal, uncontrolled pain, and an early discharge against advice; the second misses preeclampsia and abruption. Keep the MOUD, call anesthesia early, and work up the blood pressure as if no drug were involved.

UNCERTAIN POINTS

  1. Avoiding labetalol in acute stimulant toxicity is a conservative local choice: ASAM/AAAP 2024 conditionally allows a combined alpha/beta agent (labetalol, carvedilol) as an adjunct after benzodiazepines and names dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers for hypertensive emergency; the unopposed-alpha concern rests on limited evidence (review, J Cardiovasc Pharmacol Ther 2017).
  2. Stimulant withdrawal: direct fetal data are lacking (expert consensus/very low certainty); the house fetal-monitoring rule above is not an ACOG statement.
  3. CHS in pregnancy: haloperidol and capsaicin evidence is nonpregnant RCT/very low certainty; pregnancy data are case reports.
  4. Low-dose buprenorphine initiation for fentanyl use: expert consensus and case-series evidence; schedules vary by institution.
  5. The stimulant-toxicity lorazepam dose (see DOSES) is a house dose (no single guideline dose); CIWA lorazepam and clonidine doses: per unit order sets.
