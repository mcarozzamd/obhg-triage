Doses, regimens and drug amounts live only on the companion card. This algorithm lists none. Every treatment node says "see ANT-SUD card". Nothing typed into any cue is saved.

START: IN LABOR / ADMITTED, OR OB ED TRIAGE? Choose the path that matches how she presents. Unresponsive, RR <10, apnea or pulseless at any point → node 0 now.

0. Overdose / arrest (either path)

  • Pulseless → SYS-04 (maternal cardiac arrest) now.
  • Breathing poorly with suspected opioid → ventilate, naloxone titrated to breathing (see ANT-SUD card), rapid response, attending now. Observe after naloxone (shorter-acting than many opioids). Viable GA → EFM once the mother is stable.
  • Then continue on the labor path (node L) or the OB ED path (node 1).

L. In labor / admitted (labor, ROM, induction, scheduled cesarean, antepartum or postpartum inpatient)

  1. On MOUD (buprenorphine or methadone)? Verify dose and last dose (Texas PMP / prescriber / OTP). CONTINUE the home dose — never hold for labor, NPO or cesarean; never taper acutely. Dose details: see ANT-SUD card.
  2. Early anesthesia consult (OUD/MOUD, recent meth/cocaine, alcohol withdrawal).
  3. Labor pain: neuraxial preferred, early. NO nalbuphine or butorphanol in OUD/MOUD. Expect higher opioid needs — do not under-treat.
  4. Cesarean: continue MOUD + neuraxial opioid + scheduled multimodal analgesia ± abdominal wall block or extended epidural (see ANT-SUD card).
  5. BP ≥140/90 in a stimulant user → preeclampsia until proven otherwise → ANT-04 in parallel. House practice: recent cocaine/meth with HTN or agitation → lorazepam first-line; severe-range BP → nifedipine or hydralazine as needed (see ANT-SUD card); no beta-blockers, including labetalol, during acute toxicity.
  6. Recent meth/cocaine → tell anesthesia (hemodynamic instability, vasopressor choice); abruption watch (pain, bleeding, tachysystole, Category II/III → LND-01); platelets/fibrinogen before neuraxial if abruption suspected.
  7. Alcohol withdrawal (CIWA-Ar) → treat on L&D (short-acting benzodiazepine near delivery, thiamine — see ANT-SUD card); tell peds.
  8. Opioid withdrawal (COWS) → treat promptly; continue/up-dose MOUD (see ANT-SUD card).
  9. Notify peds/NICU on admission → node N. Social work/case management consult on admission → node S.
  10. Postpartum → node P.

1. Universal first step (every OBED patient)

  • Vitals with CSM maternal early warning tool (thresholds: use CSM tool); GA/viability; FHT Doppler (<20 wk) or EFM/toco (viable). Contractions, bleeding, ROM, abdominal pain or abnormal FHR → L&D evaluation (abruption/PTL) in parallel.
  • Red flags → attending now: severe-range BP, chest pain, arrhythmia/wide QRS, seizure, hyperthermia, stroke signs, severe agitation/psychosis, suicidal ideation, delirium/hallucinations, abruption signs, Category II–III FHR, fever with injection use (→ SYS-03 screen).
  • Verbal screen, every patient: 4Ps, NIDA Quick Screen, or CRAFFT (≤26 y). Ask: substance(s), route, last use, fentanyl, MOUD (name/dose/prescriber/OTP/last dose), alcohol pattern, prior withdrawal seizures/DTs, mood/suicidality, IPV, housing.
  • UDS? Only for a clinical question AND with informed consent (why, right to refuse, consequences of a positive) → node 2.
  • Then by substance: meth/stimulant intoxication → 3; stimulant withdrawal/"crash" → 4; opioids → 5; cocaine → 6; alcohol → 7; cannabis/CHS → 8. Polysubstance → open every node that applies; treat the most dangerous withdrawal first (alcohol/benzodiazepine > opioid > stimulant).

2. Urine drug testing — consent

  • House practice: test only with informed consent and a clinical indication (AMS, unexplained HTN/tachycardia, abruption, seizure, overdose). No indication → do not test; the verbal screen is the screen.
  • Indication present → consent conversation documented → test. Unable to consent (unresponsive) → test for emergency care; disclose afterward.
  • Positive immunoassay = presumptive → send confirmatory test (GC/MS or LC-MS/MS) before it is used for any report or non-urgent decision. Negative does not exclude use (fentanyl, buprenorphine, methadone need specific assays).
  • Positive result → social work consult (node S). A positive test alone, including cannabis, is not by itself a report → node T.

3. Methamphetamine / amphetamine — intoxication

  • HTN? → work up and treat as preeclampsia (ANT-04), not "just meth." House practice: lorazepam first-line; severe-range → nifedipine or hydralazine as needed (see ANT-SUD card); no beta-blockers, including labetalol; magnesium if preeclampsia with severe features is possible.
  • Agitation / hyperadrenergic → calm room, lorazepam first-line (see ANT-SUD card); psychosis unresolved → antipsychotic + psychiatry; hyperthermia → cool.
  • ECG; troponin if chest pain.
  • Viable GA → continuous EFM while hypertensive/intoxicated; abruption watch; growth US if none in 3–4 wk.
  • Admit if severe BP, preeclampsia not excluded, abruption concern, psychosis, suicidality, unable to care for self. Otherwise → node 9 when sober and normotensive.

4. Stimulant withdrawal ("crash") — including third-trimester methamphetamine

  • Hypersomnia, fatigue, hunger, dysphoria, craving. Check it is not opioid/sedative oversedation (RR, SpO2, pupils, arousability).
  • Suicidality screen with the CSM tool (e.g., C-SSRS) — every patient. Positive → sitter per CSM policy + psychiatry; do not discharge.
  • No FDA-approved medication — supportive care: sleep, fluids, nutrition, symptom relief, psychosocial support (symptom adjuncts: see ANT-SUD card).
  • Fetus: withdrawal itself is not known to harm the fetus; the risks are return to use, HTN/abruption, poor nutrition, self-harm. House practice: viable GA → NST on presentation; continuous EFM if recent use, HTN, contractions, bleeding or pain; growth US + fluid if none in 3–4 wk; no routine weekly testing for exposure alone. Do not deliver for withdrawal alone.
  • Screen for co-existing opioid/alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal (these ARE dangerous) → nodes 5/7.
  • Disposition → node 9.

5. Opioids — OUD, MOUD, withdrawal

  • On MOUD? → continue the verified dose; never taper or stop acutely (see ANT-SUD card). On methadone → do not switch to buprenorphine in pregnancy.
  • Withdrawal (COWS)? Treat promptly — untreated withdrawal may cause fetal distress/preterm labor. Rhinorrhea, yawning, piloerection = opioid; mydriasis/sweats/agitation alone may be stimulant.
  • Untreated OUD, wants treatment → start buprenorphine. House practice: OBED may start buprenorphine. COWS >8 and no recent fentanyl → standard start in OBED for discharge (see ANT-SUD card). Fentanyl use → low-dose (overlap) start → ADMIT for inpatient initiation (see ANT-SUD card); addiction medicine/pharmacy if available. Precipitated withdrawal → rescue per ANT-SUD card. UDS is not required to start.
  • Detox/medically supervised withdrawal → not recommended over MOUD.
  • No nalbuphine/butorphanol in OUD/MOUD. House practice: enter them in the EHR allergy/intolerance field.
  • Viable GA → EFM while in withdrawal.
  • Disposition → node 9 with a naloxone kit in hand and a buprenorphine bridge to confirmed follow-up.

6. Cocaine — intoxication

  • Abdominal pain or bleeding = abruption until proven otherwise → L&D, continuous EFM, CBC/fibrinogen/coags.
  • Agitation/tachycardia/HTN → lorazepam first-line (see ANT-SUD card).
  • House practice: no beta-blockers, including labetalol, acutely. Severe-range BP → nifedipine or hydralazine as needed (see ANT-SUD card).
  • Chest pain → ECG, troponin, nitroglycerin for ischemia, cardiology. Wide QRS/arrhythmia → poison center + cardiology.
  • Disposition → admit for abruption concern, ongoing HTN, chest pain; otherwise node 9.

7. Alcohol

  • Counsel: no known safe amount; FASD.
  • Withdrawal? → CIWA-Ar (nausea, headache, anxiety, insomnia count as withdrawal, not pregnancy).
  • House practice: any pregnant patient in alcohol withdrawal → ADMIT. Benzodiazepines first-line; short-acting near term/preterm risk; no valproate; thiamine (before or with glucose; never delay glucose) — see ANT-SUD card.
  • Seizure, hallucinations, DTs → attending now, ICU consideration.
  • Viable GA → EFM during active withdrawal. Peds told if treated near delivery.

8. Cannabis / cannabinoid hyperemesis (CHS)

  • Counsel: no safe amount, no medical indication; ask what it is treating (nausea, anxiety, sleep, pain) → alternatives.
  • Cyclic vomiting + daily use + relief with hot showers → consider CHS (vs hyperemesis gravidarum → ANT-08 for fluids/electrolytes/ketones).
  • Usual antiemetics + fluids → if refractory: house practice: topical capsaicin and/or haloperidol may be used in pregnancy, ECG/QTc first — see ANT-SUD card. Hot showers OK. Definitive treatment = stopping cannabis.
  • A positive cannabis UDS alone is not by itself grounds for a report → node T.
  • Breastfeeding: discourage use; continued use is not a contraindication (shared decision-making).

9. OBED disposition and discharge

  • Admit: severe BP or preeclampsia not excluded; abruption concern; overdose needing observation beyond OBED; any alcohol withdrawal; low-dose buprenorphine start; suicidality; psychosis; unable to care for self; preterm labor.
  • Home when: sober, vitals normal, preeclampsia excluded (if BP was up), reassuring FHR, suicidality screen negative, safety plan, prenatal follow-up booked.
  • Every OUD patient: naloxone kit in hand + overdose teaching + buprenorphine/OTP follow-up confirmed.
  • Every positive screen: social work/case management (node S) before discharge when available; resources given (FindTreatment.gov, SAMHSA helpline, 988, Texas OSAR).
  • Return precautions: bleeding, abdominal pain, contractions, leaking fluid, decreased FM, headache/vision change, chest pain, shortness of breath, seizure, thoughts of self-harm, withdrawal symptoms, overdose (call 911, use naloxone).

N. Neonatal team / NICU notification (before delivery)

  • Tell peds/NICU: substances, last use, MOUD + dose, alcohol, benzodiazepines, UDS results and consent status, breastfeeding plan.
  • Opioid exposure including MOUD → NOWS expected and treatable; Eat, Sleep, Console with rooming-in, skin-to-skin, parental presence.
  • Newborn testing = peds/NICU per CSM policy with parental consent (ACOG).
  • Breastfeeding: stable MOUD → encouraged; ongoing meth/cocaine, nonprescribed opioids, or heavy alcohol → avoid; cannabis → counsel, not a contraindication. Look-back (ABM #21): breastfeeding recommended when nonprescribed use has stopped by delivery.

S. Social work / case management

  • Consult on every positive screen/known SUD/MOUD — on admission, not at discharge. Always consult case management for hospital and community policy.
  • Plan of Safe Care: treatment follow-up, prenatal/postpartum + pediatric follow-up, naloxone, safe sleep, housing/food/transport, ECI if indicated, supports.
  • Community: FindTreatment.gov, SAMHSA helpline, 988, Texas OSAR, local OTP/buprenorphine prescribers, Arkansas resources if applicable (case management).

T. Texas reporting (statute summary, not legal advice — case management/social work for hospital and community policy)

  • During pregnancy: substance use alone is not a mandated report to law enforcement; Tex. Fam. Code ch. 261 applies to a child (after birth).
  • After birth: a professional with reasonable cause to believe a child is abused/neglected reports to DFPS within 48 h (cannot delegate). Newborn substance exposure → social work + DFPS intake/CAPTA Plan of Safe Care process. Prescribed MOUD is excluded from the "born addicted" statutory definition.
  • DFPS: a positive drug test alone is not evidence of abuse or neglect.

P. Postpartum (inpatient and ≤12 wk)

  • Continue MOUD; watch for oversedation as metabolism normalizes (contact prescriber/OTP before any dose change).
  • Scheduled acetaminophen + NSAID; limited opioid prescribing; naloxone kit at discharge.
  • MOUD/OTP follow-up confirmed before discharge — postpartum is the highest overdose-risk window.
  • Depression + suicidality screen; contraception including immediate postpartum LARC offered; Plan of Safe Care complete; lactation support.
